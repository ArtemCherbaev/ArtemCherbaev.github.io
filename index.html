About me

Why I test the way I do

I joined Lime Trading as its first QA engineer, on a team that had never had one. There was no process to inherit and no suite to maintain, so the first question was not what to test but what would actually go wrong, who would pay for it, and how late anyone would find out.

That is still how I decide. A bug report is cheap; the failure behind it usually is not, and the two are rarely the same size. In a trading product the expensive failures are the quiet ones — a price that renders correctly and settles wrong, a mobile build that breaks on one OS version, a message that never reaches the service downstream. Coverage follows from that rather than from the requirement list, which also means being willing to decide what not to test.

The other half of the job is the maintenance. Most automation effort goes on keeping a framework alive, which is where agentic tooling actually pays, and where I use Claude Code, MCP and agents I write myself on real work rather than as an experiment. The suite in Projects was built that way and it is public, so this is checkable rather than something you have to take on trust.

How I approach QA

Four working principles, and what each one looked like in practice.

01

A suite nobody trusts is worse than no suite

A flaky test is a defect in the test code, not background noise to be retried away. Rebuilding the suite in Playwright, I replaced every sleep with explicit state polling and moved test data setup onto the API, because the suite had to be reliable enough to gate a release before anyone would let it.

02

Testing belongs inside the pipeline, not after it

A quality gate that blocks a failing build is worth more than a report nobody opens. Smoke ran on every pull request as a merge gate, full regression nightly and pre-release, and per-service suites on deploy to staging — all in Docker against Kubernetes test environments.

03

Automate the maintenance, not only the tests

Most automation effort is spent keeping a framework alive rather than writing new coverage. Bringing Claude Code, MCP and Cursor into test generation, log analysis and defect triage took each regression cycle from about four hours of that work to an hour and a half.

04

Own the strategy, not just the tickets

Deciding what to test, in which layer, and what to stop testing is the part that needs judgement rather than throughput. I reviewed requirements and acceptance criteria before development started and resolved ambiguity at the design stage, which is far cheaper than finding it in QA.
