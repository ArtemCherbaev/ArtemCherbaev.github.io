About me

Why I test the way I do

I came to QA through building and running software, and stayed because I liked being the person who asks what happens when this fails.

Early on I measured myself the way most testers do, by how many bugs I could open. That stops working quickly. A bug report is cheap, the failure behind it usually is not, and the two are rarely the same size. So the questions I ask now are what a failure would cost, who ends up paying for it, and how late it would surface. Coverage follows from those answers rather than from the requirement list, which also means being willing to decide what not to test.

I write my own agents and skills in TypeScript rather than only prompting someone else's. The suite in Projects was built that way and it is public, so this is checkable rather than something you have to take on trust.

How I approach QA

Four working principles, and what each one looks like in practice.

01

A suite nobody trusts is worse than no suite

A flaky test is a defect in the test code, not background noise to be retried away. I treat stability as an acceptance criterion, on the same footing as the feature itself, and hold new tests to it before they are merged.

02

Testing belongs inside the pipeline, not after it

A quality gate that blocks a failing build is worth more than a report nobody opens. API coverage sits next to the UI suites inside CI/CD, so a broken contract stops the pipeline instead of surfacing in someone's release notes.

03

Automate the maintenance, not only the tests

Most automation effort goes on keeping a framework alive rather than writing new coverage. That is where agentic tooling actually pays, and where I use Claude Code, Playwright MCP and agents I write myself, on real projects rather than as an experiment.

04

Own the strategy, not just the tickets

Deciding what to test, in which layer, and what to stop testing is the part that needs a senior engineer. I have owned test strategy in Agile teams, including the decision to drop coverage that was no longer earning its maintenance.
